# User Access Review — BrightPath Digital Services Ltd.

**Project type:** Fictional GRC portfolio case study  
**Focus:** Access review process, decision documentation, and remediation tracking

## Objective
Demonstrate a structured approach to validating whether user permissions match job responsibilities, recording review decisions, and tracking approved changes through to verification.

## Scenario
BrightPath Digital Services Ltd. is fictional. The example review considers an accountant who can view and edit financial records. Viewing is known to be required; the need for editing has not yet been validated. The correct initial response is to investigate and obtain authorised confirmation—not to assume a control failure.

## Method
1. Confirm scope, review period, roles, and approval authority.
2. Obtain a current account/permission listing from an authorised source.
3. Validate the account population and compare permissions with job responsibilities.
4. Ask the appropriate owner to resolve unclear business needs.
5. Record Keep, Modify, Remove, or Investigate decisions with rationale.
6. Obtain required approval before changes.
7. Track implementation, verify results, and retain evidence.
8. Escalate unresolved issues under the organisation’s process.

## Sample of the User Access Review

The following illustrates how user accounts and permissions can be reviewed to determine whether access remains appropriate.

| User | Department | System Access | Review Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| John | Finance | Accounting application | Keep | Access required for assigned duties |
| Sarah | Marketing | Customer database | Investigate | Business justification requires confirmation |
| David | IT | Administrator account | Modify | Privileged access requires review |
| Former Employee | Operations | Corporate email | Remove | Employment has ended |

### Access Review Decisions

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Keep | Access is appropriate and remains necessary |
| Modify | Access needs to be adjusted |
| Remove | Access is no longer required |
| Investigate | Additional information is needed before deciding |

## Evidence considerations
Potential evidence includes an account/permission export, job responsibilities, documented owner approval, a change ticket, and verification of resulting permissions. Never include passwords, tokens, or confidential company data.

## Framework reference
Broadly related to NIST CSF 2.0 Protect (PR.AA: identity management, authentication, and access control). This is a high-level mapping, not a formal compliance assessment or certification claim.

## Limitations
All company names, users, systems, and records are fictional. No live environment was accessed, no permissions were changed, and no real audit was conducted. This portfolio artifact demonstrates a proposed process and documentation approach only.
